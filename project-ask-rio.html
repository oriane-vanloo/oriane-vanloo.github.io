Meet Rio, Rome2Rio's new AI travel assistant

Ask Rio answers the questions that come up when you plan a trip: how to get there, where to stay, what to do and when to go. Answers come from timetables and booking data, and every one ends with a way to keep going.

A trip is a pile of small questions. How do I get there? Where should I stay? Is August a terrible time to go? Rome2Rio already holds the answers. They just live on different pages.

Ask Rio brings them into one conversation. This is the story of how we got there: a first version I'm glad we didn't ship, the call to build the real thing, and a toucan who needed a body of his own.

What Rio can help with

Ask in your own words or start from a suggestion. Rio covers the four things people ask most when they plan.

Getting from A to BRoutes, times and prices
Where to stayAreas, and how long people stay
Things to doSights and activities
When to goBusy and quiet times

It started as a form

The first idea was modest. Take the fields people already fill in on Rome2Rio, from, to, when, and deconstruct them into a chat. Rio asks one question at a time. You pick a topic, type a place, and get an answer. If a place could be two places, say Newcastle in the UK or in Australia, Rio asks which one you mean.

It was a deliberately dumb chatbot, with far fewer scenarios to think about.

The first version: Rio asks, you answer, one field at a time.

But we wanted to build the real deal

A chat that only pretends to be one could tell us whether people like four buttons. It couldn't tell us what happens when someone types a real question, which is where an assistant earns its keep.

So we decided to build Ask Rio properly and learn from it. What made that feel safe was a simple idea: Rio doesn't need to know everything. He needs to know where to send you.

"If Rio can't help with something, there's always a part of Rome2Rio that can."

Ask in your own words

Type a question and the answer comes back in the shape that fits it: a spread of nights for how long to stay, cards to compare costs with a map, a month-by-month chart for the best time to visit. Every answer says where it comes from, whether that's a live timetable or a year of booking data, and has thumbs up and down so Rio keeps getting better.

Always a way forward

Open Ask Rio and there are a few questions to start from, like Where should I stay in Positano? or Fastest way from Rome?. Every answer finishes by pointing you to the next step in Rome2Rio: how to get there, hotels, things to do. Ask about something outside travel and Rio steers you back to what he does best, with a couple of questions to try.

Giving Rio a face

An assistant you talk to needs someone to talk to. Rio is a toucan, and he began in 2D, drawn in layers so every part could move on its own: wings, tail, eyelid, beak. Drag the slider to build him in the order he was drawn.

All layers

Firing each mood in Rive.

In Rive he runs on a small state machine with six moods, so he reacts to what the assistant is doing.

HelloGreets you when the chat opens.
ThinkingHolds while Rio works out an answer.
AnswerPerks up when it lands.
SuccessCelebrates a good result.
NoShakes his head when something goes wrong.
SleepyDozes off if you leave him alone.

Just enough personality

The 2D toucan did the job, but it felt a little retro. I wanted Rio to be more personable, so I went 3D and experimented with mesh, textures and shading.

What came out is a Rio with just enough personality. He doesn't steal the stage, but he's friendly enough to make you want to start a chat. Three steps got us there: the shape, the surface, then the manners.

1 · Mesh and rigA sculpted toucan with a skeleton, so his head, beak and wings can be posed.
2 · TextureColour, a glossy beak and a lively eye turn grey clay into Rio.
Says helloThinksAnswersCelebratesShakes its head noNapsFollows your cursor
3 · MannerismsThe same six moods as the 2D version, plus eyes that follow you around the screen.

Where we go from here

Ask Rio isn't in the product yet, so there are no numbers to share, and I won't make any up. Stay tuned. When it launches, this is what I'll be watching:

  • What people actually ask, and which topics they reach for first
  • Where answers send people next: hotels, things to do, routes
  • The questions Rio can't answer yet, and what they tell us to build next
  • What the thumbs down are telling us
