Rome2Rio · 2026 H2

Know your customer

Rome2Rio couldn't tell when the same person came back. I led the strategy to get more of our travellers to log in, and logged-in users now return at 9 times the rate of anonymous ones.

Product strategy User retention Account systems

My role

Product Design Lead

Stakeholders

Leadership, product, engineering, marketing

Outcome

64% return rate (logged-in vs 7.2% anonymous)

The problem

Most sessions were anonymous so cookies were the only way to recognise anyone, and cookies don't follow people across devices. Someone who researched a trip on desktop and came back on mobile looked like two different people. We had no real idea who our repeat customers were and nothing to offer them like saved trips or price alerts.

The insight

Logged-in users returned 64% of the time each week against 7.2% for anonymous users, which is 9 times more. The people we wanted were already coming back. We just couldn't see them or give them a reason to stay, and an account fixes both.

The approach in four parts

1
Awareness Show sign-up where people already have a trip in mind, like search results, trip confirmation and saved pages.
2
Frictionless sign-in Google and Omio single sign-on and in-app account linking, so signing in takes a tap for anyone who is ready.
3
Value exchange Give people a clear reason to log in, like saving trips, price drop alerts, traveller profiles and route Q&A.
4
Data quality Verify emails, ask for preferences and spot gaps, so each sign-up improves everything we send that person afterwards.

What I built

Sign-up and onboarding – Redesigned the whole account creation flow. Google and Omio come first with email as a fallback, and traveller details and preferences are collected as part of signing up instead of as a separate chore.

Core features that drive value – Save trips for later, price drop alerts for when fares fall, traveller profiles that pre-fill names and ages in every booking, and Q&A where people ask route questions and share tips.

Email system – Built a follow-up sequence that points out what people might be missing, like saved trips, empty preferences or unanswered questions, and slowly improves each account over time.

The features in action

Save trips People bookmark routes they're considering and come back to them months later when they're ready to book.
Price alerts We email when a saved trip gets cheaper, which gives people a reason to come back at the moment they're most likely to book.
Traveller profiles Passenger details are saved once and filled into every booking, which means fewer steps and more completed bookings.
Questions & answers People ask route questions like bike rules, toilet stops and luggage allowance. Other travellers answer and Rome2Rio learns from the answers.

"Logged-in users return at 9 times the rate of anonymous users. So the real question was how to keep the customers we already had."

From an insight to a funded mission

This started as an insight with no brief. I built the case through research then ran a workshop with leadership and product on what travellers gain from an account and what holds them back. It is now a funded company mission with a measurable target.

I picked logged-in session share as the measure instead of accounts created because it shows whether travellers actually come back, and that is where the revenue is. I lead the mission with my own pod of design, frontend, backend, app and data, with a shared ranking, a Kanban board and ways of working that fit around dependencies between teams.

Results

64% Weekly return rate for logged-in users (vs 7.2% anonymous)
+3% Increase in active logged-in session share of total web traffic
✓ A base for personalisation, retention and decisions backed by data
