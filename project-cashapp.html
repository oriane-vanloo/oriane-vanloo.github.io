Cash App Bill Split

I designed a working bill-splitting flow for Cash App in 3 days. It uses Square's point of sale so people can split a purchase with friends in the app straight after paying, with no Splitwise or Venmo and no maths.

Product Design Rapid Prototyping Payments Fintech

My role

Solo designer

Format

Hack Week · 3 days

Result

Added to product pipeline by Head of Design

The problem with splitting bills

Splitting a bill with friends is an everyday hassle that nobody has really solved. You pay then you chase, or everyone installs Splitwise, or someone quietly eats the cost. It's awkward socially as much as it is technically.

Cash App already had peer-to-peer payments and Square already had the merchant point of sale. So if someone pays with Cash App at a Square terminal, why not let them split the bill right there in the app?

The solution

A bill-splitting flow built into Cash App for the moment after someone pays through the Square integration. They pick contacts, split by amount or by item, check the split and send requests without leaving the app.

I focused on three things. Picking contacts had to be fast so it uses people's existing Cash App contacts. The split had to be clear so everyone can see what they owe and what they get back. The end of the flow had to feel good.

How it came together

1
Audited existing solutions I looked at how Venmo and Splitwise handle splitting and where they cause friction, like switching apps, typing things in by hand and waiting to be paid back. That told me what better looked like inside Cash App.
2
Mapped the POS integration entry point I chose the trigger, which is the moment after someone pays at a Square terminal with Cash App. From there I mapped every state that follows, from a solo purchase to a split between two people or many as well as item-level splits.
3
Designed the full flow I worked with a remote engineering team in Canada to design and prototype the whole experience across every kind of split. I covered the edge cases too, like adding someone halfway through, changing amounts and contacts who aren't on Cash App.
4
Delivered a working prototype in 3 days I presented a working interactive prototype instead of a concept deck. Engineers could test it for feasibility straight away and product could use it to pitch to stakeholders.

"Every person who uses the feature is likely to bring in more people, which helps both Cash App and the Square merchants who accept it."

Why this matters beyond the feature

The business case had a few layers. A bill-splitting feature is a growth tool as well as a convenience. Every request sent to someone who isn't on Cash App invites them to join, and every Square merchant who turns on the integration sees more Cash App payments.

It creates a loop where merchants gain as more of their customers use Cash App and customers gain as more merchants accept it. That only works because the feature sits in the moment after paying instead of being a separate tool.
